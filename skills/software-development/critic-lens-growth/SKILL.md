# Critic Lens: Advertising, Marketing, Copy, and Growth

## When to Use

Load alongside `persona-skeptic` when the artifact is ad creative, landing copy, a positioning statement, a pricing page, an email, a launch plan, or a growth experiment. This lens separates what the maker likes from what actually works, and hunts the claims that will get David sued or refunded.

## The first question: does it fit the brief

Before taste, check fit. A beautiful ad that misses the brief is a failure.
- Does the key message and tone match the brief's objective and audience.
- Is the takeaway singular. One ad, one idea. If a reader cannot repeat the offer back in one sentence, it failed (clarity beats cleverness every time).

## Substantiation and legal risk (the money finding)

- Every claim on a paid or public surface must be substantiable. "Proven", "guaranteed", "best", "most", "free" have legal definitions and evidentiary bars.
- Unsubstantiated comparative and superiority claims are FTC risk (US) and a refund and chargeback risk. Flag each as OVERSTATED or UNBACKED with the substantiation that would clear it.
- Testimonials and results carry disclosure requirements. "Results not typical" is not a fix for an atypical claim.

## Positioning and UVP attack
- **Clarity over cleverness.** If the value proposition needs a second read, cut it.
- **The one-sentence test.** Can a reader explain the offer to a friend after one read.
- **Differentiation.** What does this say that the competitor cannot say truthfully. If the copy would work with the competitor's logo swapped, it is generic.
- **Audience specificity.** "Everyone" is no one. Name the ICP and the job to be done.

## Performance-creative reality check
- Hook in the first 3 seconds or first line, or the attention is gone.
- One CTA, one intent. Multiple competing CTAs convert none.
- Does the creative promise match the destination. Bait headline plus mismatched landing page is a conversion killer and a trust breach.
- Format fit: does the creative match the placement (feed vs story vs search intent vs email subject).

## What the growth skeptic always demands

- **The mechanism.** Why will this convert. "It looks good" is not a mechanism. Name the psychological or economic driver.
- **The number's source.** Projected revenue, CAC, or lift must trace to a model or a real cohort, not optimism. Cross-check with critic-lens-claims when numbers get heavy.
- **The honest comparison.** Against the top competitor the customer would actually weigh, not a strawman.

## Output additions (append to the core contract)
- **Substantiation ledger.** Every claim, its verdict label, the evidence bar it must clear.
- **Brief-fit score.** Does it hit the objective, the audience, the single takeaway.
- **The swap test.** Would this work under a competitor's logo. Yes means generic.

## Failure modes this lens exists to catch

- Maker-taste review, not audience review. "I like it" is not a finding.
- Clever headline that fails the one-sentence test.
- Unsubstantiated superiority claim waiting to be a legal problem.
- Creative-to-landing mismatch. Promise the thing the page delivers.
- Growth number with no cohort or model behind it.
