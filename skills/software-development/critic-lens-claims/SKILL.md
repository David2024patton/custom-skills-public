# Critic Lens: Claims, Research, and Data

## When to Use

This lens is a checklist every critic carries: in a Critic family run, all five full-spectrum critics load it alongside `persona-skeptic` and work it in full, on every artifact, whatever the artifact type. On artifacts of its shape (a research report, competitor analysis, market claim, metric, projection, or any number-heavy document), this lens's findings lead the merged report. This is the original Skeptic's home turf, weaponized with fact-check and statistics method.

## The fact-check method (per load-bearing claim)

1. **Find the original source**, not the aggregator. Trace the number up the citation chain until you hit whoever generated it. If three outlets cite one study, that is one source, not three (circular reporting).
2. **Check the source actually says what the work claims.** Misquotation is the most common subtle defect. Quote the source's own words back.
3. **Assess the source.** Primary (the body that generated the data) vs secondary (someone reporting it). Peer-reviewed vs blog vs vendor whitepaper vs press release.
4. **Date it.** Numbers move. Anything past its shelf life is OUTDATED, not TRUE.

## Statistical scrutiny (when the work leans on data)

- **Sample size and power.** A 3 percent lift on 40 users is noise. Ask for n and the confidence interval.
- **p-hacking and multiple comparisons.** If 20 metrics were tested and one is significant, that is the expected false positive, not a finding.
- **Selection and survivorship.** Who is missing from the dataset. Did failures get dropped.
- **Correlation dressed as causation.** "Users who X churn less" is not "doing X stops churn". Demand the mechanism or the experiment.
- **Base rate neglect.** A conversion rate without the denominator is meaningless.
- **Overfitting.** A model or rule tuned and evaluated on the same data proves nothing. Demand a holdout.
- **Recomputed math.** Recalculate every total, percentage, and conversion from the stated inputs. Arithmetic errors hide in confident documents.

## Vendor and interest tagging

Every figure generated by a party with a financial interest is a VENDOR SOURCED claim. Name the seller and the interest in one line. Industry reports, pricing pages, and growth case studies are the usual suspects. "According to the vendor's own benchmark" is not the same as "independently measured".

## Overstatement heuristics

Flag: superlatives (best, leading, proven, guaranteed), round numbers presented as precise, 100 percent claims, and any hedge removed from the original source (a "may" became a "will").

## What this lens always demands

- **Provenance on every number:** source URL, observation date, method tag (live fetch, crawl, doc, secondary, estimate).
- **The gap analysis.** What did the work leave out: the failing competitor, the hidden cost, the untested assumption.
- **The parity reality check.** For competitor claims, does the cited source actually show what is claimed, verbatim.

## Output additions (append to the core contract)
- **Circular-source map.** Where multiple citations collapse to one origin.
- **Recomputed figures.** The work's math vs your recomputation, side by side.
- **Gap list.** Missing competitors, costs, and failure modes.

## Failure modes this lens exists to catch

- Aggregator cited as primary. Vendor benchmark dressed as independent measurement.
- Circular reporting mistaken for corroboration.
- Stale figure presented as current. Rounded number presented as measured.
- Significance claimed from an underpowered or cherry-picked sample.

## Refinement pass additions (P-10, P-11, P-13)

- **Probe protocol (CS-09).** A claim about what a system does is verified by single-variable probes, then rewritten as a Given/When/Then rule with probe evidence attached. A claim that cannot be probed is UNBACKED, with the settling probe named; it enters the unknowns ledger.
- **Parity table (CS-10).** This lens's output is the fixed parity table: claim, where stated, what would prove it, observed evidence, verdict label (TRUE, FALSE, UNBACKED, VENDOR SOURCED, OVERSTATED, OUTDATED). Every row carries an evidence ID or an UNBACKED label with its settling probe named. For competitor-parity claims, the cited source's own words sit beside the claim.
- **Claims diff (CS-02).** Claimed but not observed is a finding against the claim; observed but not claimed is a finding against the documentation, with an intent check.
- **Bounded negatives (CS-05).** Every "no X found" states its examined scope.
- **Not tested.** This lens's return ends with a Not tested list.
