# Query Playbook

## Seed searches

- Open with 3 to 5 distinct searches, not rephrasings of one search. One for the definition and official anchor, one for data and measurement, one for real world examples, one for the strongest criticism, one for the current state.
- Harvest the vocabulary the results use. Official names, program codes, version numbers, and the terms critics use become the next round of queries.
- Map the dimensions before diving. Write the subtopics, stakeholders, and open disagreements down in the run notes.

## Deepen each dimension

- Add an authority hint when it helps: the agency name, the journal or venue, the standard number, the dataset name, the company filing.
- Add a content type hint: case study, statistics, benchmark, guidance, eligibility, pricing, release notes, postmortem.
- Follow citation chains. When a strong source cites the study or document a claim rests on, go read that document instead of citing the summary.
- Follow entity chains. Every new named program, product, person, or rule that could change the answer earns one targeted search.

## Temporal precision

Use the actual current date and match the precision to the intent.

- Today or just released: search with month, day, and year, in numeric and written forms, plus the word today.
- This week: search the week range and the words this week.
- Recently or latest: search the current month and year.
- Trends or this year: search the current year.
- A rule, price, or version that may have changed: search the item name plus the current year, and prefer the result the publisher itself dates most recently.

## Counterevidence searches

Run at least one of these before finishing any deep run.

- The topic plus limitations, criticism, problems, fails, or does not work.
- The headline claim plus debunked, retracted, corrected, or overstated.
- The product or program plus complaints, bugs, security issues, or discontinued.
- The statistic itself in quotes, to find its original source and age.

## Stop conditions

Hop counts in the main skill are planning estimates only, never quotas and never a stop or continue rule on their own. Stop on evidence sufficiency, not on a number.

Stop searching when the binding condition is met, and say so in the method note:

- Every material claim has adequate evidence (for high impact claims, a Tier A source plus independent corroboration), contradictions are resolved or labeled, and additional research is unlikely to change the conclusion.

Any of these also supports stopping when the binding condition above is substantially met:

- The last two hops changed nothing about the conclusion.
- The remaining gaps are named, labeled with an uncertainty tag, and unlikely to close with the sources that exist.
- The planning estimate for the effort level is spent, every material claim is recorded in the claim ledger with a status, and the open questions are written down for the user to see. If a material claim is still unsupported at that point, it is cut or labeled, not carried at full confidence.
