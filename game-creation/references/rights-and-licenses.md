# Rights and licenses: gate zero

Nothing gets built, published, or monetized until this gate clears. It applies to original games that borrow anything, to revivals, and to emulation.

## The rights pass

- Establish who owns the original game's name, art, music, and code before recreating any of it. A dead game is not an ownerless game.

- Recreate rather than redistribute: write new code and draw new assets from research. Never ship or repo original ROMs, BIOS images, firmware dumps, art packs, or extracted assets.

- Mirror and archive original material for research only, keep it out of the product repo's shipped content, and say plainly in the repo what is research material.

- Emulator development and interoperability copying have court support in the US (the Connectix and Bleem cases), but that protection covers building a compatible program, not distributing games, BIOS images, or original art. These lines are summaries, not legal advice, and they vary by territory, so recheck before a commercial release.

## The license audit

- Audit every reused core, engine, library, tool, font, and asset at the exact version used, and record it in a license ledger in the repo: name, version, license, what it allows, what it forbids.

- Read for the traps specifically: copyleft obligations that reach your own code, non-commercial clauses that block ads, sales, and bundling, and attribution requirements.

- A non-commercial clause discovered late can kill a finished product's business model. Check before the first build, and re-check terms before launch because licenses and engine terms change.

- Run the same audit on the engine choice itself: total license cost at expected revenue, royalty triggers, and the exit cost if terms change.
